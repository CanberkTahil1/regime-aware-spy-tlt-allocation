# R5 — Freeze, the one test run, and the write-up

## In two sentences
The frozen rules were run once on 2020-01 → 2026-09, and no layer beat plain vol-targeted SPY, 60/40, or SPY + T-bills
at matched risk. The 2022 stock–bond sell-off turned the bond layers' development edge negative. The correlation switch
the thesis rests on fired only in August 2022, too late to matter.

## What happened, step by step
- **Your 10-year row was registered first.** L5–L7 on a 10-year bond leg went into `spec/prereg.yaml` `r5_test` and
  `reports/variants.csv`, hashed in the ledger before the freeze. The rest of the test definition was registered with
  it (D5.1–D5.7).
- **The engine.**
  - `src/rspy/testwin.py` builds the whole ladder once, 1986 → 2026-09. It uses the R2/R3 functions unchanged, then
    scores only the test rows.
  - It checks that every 1990–2019 row equals the stored development books bit for bit: 35 of 35 checks pass.
- **The freeze and the guard.** `src/rspy/freeze.py` writes `spec/freeze.lock.json`, the hashes of 39 files plus the
  data. `scripts/r5.py --mode run` refuses unless:
  - the hashes match and git is clean;
  - `RSPY_TEST_RUN=run-once` is set;
  - no `reports/r5/test_run.json` exists yet.

  It writes that record as "started" before computing anything.
- **The dry run and the review.** The synthetic dry run found nothing wrong on its own. The reviewer then found that
  its fake data had zero bill rates (a pandas time-unit bug), plus three other gaps. All were fixed before the freeze.
  The placebo question became D5.8.
- **The run itself** took 65 s and 947 MB. Outputs are in `reports/r5/`; figures are in `reports/figures/R5/` and
  `reports/figures_local/R5/`. `make r5-replay` recomputes every table byte for byte.
- **The README draft** is in the working tree, uncommitted, waiting for your approval.

## Why these choices
- D5.1 (advisor): keep running the registered rules (yearly vol-model re-pick, continuous books), not a frozen
  snapshot.
- D5.2 (advisor): "matched risk" means the same thing as in development, rescaled on the test window, with the ex-ante
  line beside it.
- D5.3 (advisor): the test can't change a verdict. "Beat" needs +0.05 Sharpe and a Holm-adjusted p < 0.05.
- D5.4 (advisor): deflated-Sharpe N comes from the development register (5), with 751 as the raw companion.
- D5.5 (advisor): content-hash lock, no tag, "started" record before any number.

## The one number to remember
**0.12 vs 0.69:** the excess Sharpe of the core mix (inverse-vol + vol target, L5) and of SPY + T-bills held at the
same volatility, on the 1,695 test sessions (2020-01-02 → 2026-09-30). In development it was 0.83 against the line's
0.58.

## Concept check
- **Why ex-post matching doesn't flatter here.** The reference series y is scaled to x's excess vol:
  k = σ(x − c) / σ(y − c), and y_m = k·y + (1 − k)·c, financed above 1×. Sharpe is scale-free, so ΔSharpe barely moves.
  What matching changes is drawdowns and crisis returns. That is why the crisis table uses the ex-ante line.
- **A lagging gate vs a placebo.** If a gate is right only because it removes bonds, then removing bonds at random
  times of the same total length should do as well. In 2022 the random shuffles did better (median +4.1 pp vs the
  gate's +1.1 pp). The gate added nothing beyond "fewer bonds".

## Interview questions
1. **Your test window says nothing worked. Was the strategy wrong or the window unlucky?** Both readings fit, and 6.75
   years can't separate them. Even the best development layer needs about 30 years for 80% power. What the test does
   show is that the edge depended on long Treasuries hedging stocks. 2022 broke that, and the rule meant to detect the
   break (a 126-day correlation) first fired at the end of August 2022, after most of the year's bond loss.
2. **How do you know you didn't peek at the test before freezing?** Every rule and comparison was hashed into the
   ledger first. The lock hashes 39 code and spec files plus the data. The runner writes a "started" record before
   computing anything and refuses a second run. The window is still labelled "seen by owner in v1", because the owner
   saw 2020–25 in the old project.
3. **The trend layer did well in 2022. Why not promote it?** Because promotion happens only in development, and there
   trend lowered Sharpe by 0.17 against its parent. On the test its 2022 gain is inside the range of random timing
   shuffles. One good crisis out of two, in a window chosen after the fact, is an anecdote.

## What could be wrong
- **The bond leg is a constructed 25-year Treasury,** QA'd against TLT at a 2.06%/yr tracking error. In 2022 that error
  could matter for the size of the loss, though not its sign.
- **The test window is one regime.** A reader could fairly say the ladder was judged on its worst possible six years
  for long bonds.
