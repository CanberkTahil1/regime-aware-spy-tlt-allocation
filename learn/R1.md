# R1 — Data swap and correct accounting

## In two sentences
v2 now runs on permitted data (Ken French Hi 20 for SPY, a 25-year Treasury rebuilt from the Fed's curve for TLT, Fed
T-bills for cash) through one honest accounting function. Taking v1 from its own books to v2's one fix at a time, its
2020–25 Sharpe on a like-for-like excess basis goes from 0.83 to 0.78, and most of the old headline 1.00 was the missing
risk-free rate and same-close trading.

## What happened, step by step
- `make fetch` / `make data`: `src/rspy/data/` downloads the three sources (manifest with sha256) and builds
  `data/panel.parquet`; the bond leg is `quantkit.bonds.total_return(..., par_col=None)`, bit-identical to the sibling's
  validated construction.
- `src/rspy/accounting.py::run`: decide at close t, trade at close t+1, drift, true turnover, 3/2 bp costs, bills on cash,
  bill + 25 bp on leverage, no shorts. Checked against a plain loop in `tests/reference/accounting_ref.py`.
- `src/rspy/legacy_bridge.py::reconcile` + `make reconcile`: the eight-step table `reports/r1/reconciliation.md` and the
  data QA `reports/r1/qa.csv`.
- Figures in `reports/figures/R1/` (start with `reconciliation_by_step.png`), page in `reports/site/index.html`.

## Why these choices
- D1.1 (advisor): cash = Fed 3-month bill; leverage paid at its money-market yield + 25 bp (never cheaper than cash).
- D1.2 (advisor): 25-year bond priced off the fitted curve, exactly as validated; the published-coupon shortcut moves
  daily returns by up to 0.7%.
- D1.3 (advisor): one accounting function; Sharpe computed on the excess series.
- D1.4 (advisor): fixed step order; VIX swapped out before the data swap so no row mixes excluded and permitted data.
- D1.5 (advisor): VIX → v1's own 20-day realised vol. D1.6 (advisor): QA pass marks fixed before computing.

## The one number to remember
L1 (v1 on permitted data, charter accounting) in 2020–25 (seen in v1, disclosed): **excess Sharpe 0.78, CAGR 15.5%**,
against v1's reported Sharpe 1.00 (no risk-free rate, same-close trading, free leverage). That 0.78 is the bar R2 must
beat.

## Concept check
- **Excess Sharpe** = mean(r − r_f)/sd(r − r_f)·√252. Leaving r_f out flatters any strategy that holds cash or runs
  levered on cheap money: v1's test Sharpe drops from 1.00 to 0.83 on this change alone (S0 on both definitions).
- **True traded turnover** = Σ|w_target − w_drifted| per trade: what you actually buy and sell, including vol-scaling
  trades. v1 reported 18.7× (signal changes only); it really traded 26.6×.

## Try it yourself (5 minutes)
`make reconcile` — prints the 24-row table in ~2 s; S0 test shows CAGR 0.1631 and Sharpe 1.0033 (v1's numbers), S7
test 0.1551 / 0.7803.

## Interview questions
1. **How did you make sure the old result and the new one are comparable?** I reproduced v1 exactly first, then changed
   one thing per row in an order fixed before seeing any numbers. Each row's difference is then caused by that one
   change, and the caption says the attribution depends on the order.
2. **One of your pre-registered expectations failed. What did you do?** "Trading a day later hurts a fast signal" failed
   in 2005–14: it helped. I logged the failure before looking for a cause, found no bug, and showed that one-day timing
   has no stable sign for this signal (worst in train at lag 0, worst in validation at lag 1). I left the expectation
   failed and changed nothing.
3. **Why rebuild TLT from the Fed curve instead of using the ETF?** The ETF price data couldn't be stored under its
   terms. A 25-year constant-maturity par bond off the Fed's public-domain curve tracks TLT at 0.994 monthly correlation
   and 1.5%/yr tracking error over 2005–25, and it gives history back to 1986.

## What could be wrong
The reconciliation reads v1's own Yahoo/Cboe file for rows S0–S6 and the QA. Only statistics and figures are committed,
but whether publishing statistics derived from that file is fine under Cboe's terms is an open owner question.
