# R6 — Improvements suggested by R5

## In two sentences
Fifteen ideas suggested by R5's test results were run on both windows. Each was judged against the one rung it
changes, at matched risk, with plain-word verdicts. None beat vol-targeted SPY or SPY + T-bills on 2020–2026.
- **The 2022-inspired bond switches** (an inflation-volatility gate, trend on bonds only) were worse in development.
  On the test they did no better than random timing or a flat bond cut.
- **Only the trend rung on a shorter bond leg** beat its parent in both periods.

## What happened, step by step
- **Registered first, in its own file.**
  - `spec/prereg_r6.yaml` holds every candidate, parameter and verdict word. Its sha256 went into the ledger before any
    R6 number.
  - It is a separate file because R5's lock fingerprints `spec/prereg.yaml` (your O6.1).
  - The 15 rows are in `reports/variants.csv`, counted in the multiple-testing N.
- **New data, from the source agency.**
  - `r6lib/cpi.py` pulls CPI-U NSA from the BLS API (your O6.2, not FRED).
  - Month m is used only at the end of month m+1, a lag checked against the BLS release archive.
  - The missing October 2025 print (the 2025 lapse in appropriations) leaves the gate defined.
- **Built from the registered code.**
  - `r6lib/books.py` composes targets from `robust.build_core`, `regime` and `rungs`. Nothing under `src/rspy/` was
    edited except the page module.
  - The new pieces are the asymmetric vol-target recursion (numba, checked against a plain loop) and the one-third
    fund of books (checked against a dollar-NAV reference).
- **Evaluated and checked.**
  - `r6lib/report.py` does the evaluation; `scripts/r6.py` runs it (`make r6`).
  - It refuses to run unless every reference book is bit-identical to the stored R2/R3/R5 books (41 checks).
- **The review changed three things:**
  - **D6.6 placebo:** each switch was compared with 100 random timings and a flat cut. No timing skill shows in
    development.
  - **O6.3 wording:** a phrase for "worse in development, better in the test", and interval/power flags on every
    verdict.
  - **O6.4 Makefile:** R5's Makefile was restored byte for byte and R6 moved to `Makefile.r6`. `make freeze-verify`
    says "freeze OK" again.

## Why these choices
- **O6.1 (owner):** R6 is registered in `spec/prereg_r6.yaml`, not prereg.yaml, so R5 stays replayable.
- **O6.2 (owner):** CPI comes straight from the BLS; FRED's terms bar storage. Gold is skipped: there is no monthly
  engine.
- **D6.1 (advisor):** the slow-re-entry vol target steps up on Fridays. The EWMA version is the one allowed variant.
- **D6.2 (advisor):** the 63-day correlation gate and the inflation gate are separate one-layer rows, never combined.
- **D6.3 (advisor):** the blend is three accounts reset monthly, with the reset trade charged.
- **D6.4 (advisor):** verdicts use the charter's minimum effect: ±0.05 Sharpe, or 15% lower CDaR. The wording is fixed
  for all nine (development, test) combinations.
- **D6.5 (advisor), reversed by O6.4 (owner):** R6 runs from `Makefile.r6`, so R5's locked Makefile stays
  byte-identical.
- **D6.6 (advisor, post-hoc):** placebos and a flat cut for every switch. They can only take credit away.
- **O6.3 (owner, post-hoc):** a 10th phrase, plus clearly/power flags beside every verdict.

## The one number to remember
**+0.38 vs +0.34**: the inflation-volatility gate's Sharpe gain over its parent on 2020–2026, against the median of the
same gate with its months shuffled at random (100 times). The timing added nothing. Holding fewer bonds did the work.

## Concept check
- **Matched-risk ΔSharpe.** The reference r is rescaled with T-bills to the candidate's volatility:
  r' = k·r + (1 − k)·bill − financing, with k = σ_cand / σ_ref. Then ΔSharpe = SR(cand) − SR(r'). Without matching, an
  idea that just holds more cash (lower volatility) can look "safer" without being better.
- **Why "better only in the period that inspired it" is a weak result.** The idea was chosen because it would have
  helped in 2022. Its 2022 success is then partly a selection effect. Only the period that did not suggest it
  (1990–2019) is a fair, though in-sample, check.

## Interview questions
1. **You tested ideas on the same window that suggested them. Isn't that useless?** Not useless, but it can't confirm
   anything. I labelled the test "disclosed reuse", fixed every rule before computing, and judged each idea mainly by
   whether it also helped in 1990–2019, which did not suggest it. Four ideas helped only in 2020–26, three of them
   after losing in 1990–2019. A placebo showed the two bond switches did no better than random timing.
2. **Why did the inflation gate fail in development if inflation drives the stock–bond correlation?** It measures the
   *volatility* of monthly inflation, and that also spikes when oil swings, as in 2008. Then it removed bonds exactly
   when they hedged the equity crash: CDaR 1.59× its parent. It even missed early 2022, because 2021's prints were high
   but steady.
3. **Your asymmetric vol target cut turnover by a quarter. Why not adopt it?** Its Sharpe didn't move (±0.02), and at
   3 bp a side the saved cost is a few basis points a year. It is a reasonable implementation detail, not an
   improvement; the verdict is "no real difference".

## What could be wrong
- The "better in both periods" rows (trend on a 5- or 10-year leg) rest on one choice: the bond maturity. That choice
  was made after seeing 2022, and the 5- and 10-year legs are not checked against an ETF.
- The 10-year numbers were known before registration.
- The 5-year gain comes with a different mix: the + trend rung holds less SPY (0.18 vs 0.35) and more bonds (0.42 vs
  0.23) in development. It may be a different portfolio rather than a better one.
- Their development intervals are wide: the 10-year one includes 0.
- They still trail vol-targeted SPY on 2020–2026.
