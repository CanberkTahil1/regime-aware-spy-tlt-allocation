## D4.1 — What "v1 spread / Bollinger overlay on L5" means

- **(a) Cash-only event overlay.** Use v1's frozen signal code with v1's selected parameters (spread: window 10,
  th 2.5; Bollinger: window 10, 2 sd) on the SPY and TLT proxies, decided at close t and traded at t+1. On a day the
  signal fires, each sleeve to which v1 gives weight ≤ 0 goes to cash in L5's daily target: z > th sends SPY to cash,
  z < −th sends TLT to cash. On neutral days the book is L5 unchanged. The vol scale stays L5's, as in D3.1. L5's
  5 pp band applies, with the zero-target exit. Trade-off: it keeps only the *direction* of v1's mean-reversion view,
  but it is one layer, adds no risk, and uses the same action form as L6/L7, so the rows are comparable.
- **(b) Replace on signal days.** On a signal day, L5's target becomes v1's weights floored at 0, renormalised and scaled
  to L5's gross (100% in one sleeve). This is closer to v1's intent, but it concentrates L5's risk in a single sleeve.
- **(c) Full translation.** v1's weights floored at 0 (neutral = 100% SPY) replace L4's mix, then L5's vol target. This
  is v1's allocator re-scaled, not an overlay; it is mostly SPY, like v1.
- Sources: the charter calls these "overlays on L5" (zoo list). The "no shorts ever" and "cut to cash" vocabulary comes
  from the charter thesis. v1's state logic is cited above. Mean-reversion signals on daily spreads have short
  half-lives (Lo & MacKinlay 1990, RFS 3(2)).
- **Data check:** none. O3.1 forbids computing before registration, and signal frequency is a result.
- **Recommendation: (a).** Strongest reason: it is the only reading that is one layer with no shorts and no new risk,
  so its ablation versus L5 isolates the signal. **Pushback:** "a cash overlay throws away v1's long-TLT leg (z > th)".
  Answer: the leg a cash overlay can express is SPY→cash; the switch to TLT is (b), and it is listed.

## D4.2 — The GBM risk-side sizing row (target, features, horizon, CV, haircut)

- **(a) A LightGBM vol forecast with a 50% log-space shrink toward L5's own forecast.**
  - Model: `quantkit.ml.lightgbm` with fixed defaults, seed 0, no search.
  - Label: y_t = log realised vol of the unscaled L4 book's daily net return over sessions t+2 … t+22. That is
    21 sessions, the window a trade at t+1 earns.
  - Features, each using data ≤ t: log of L5's zoo forecast at t; log realised vol of the L4 book over 5, 21, 63 and
    252 sessions; the SPY–TLT 63-day correlation; the SPY proxy's 21-session return; the bill rate.
  - Refit: walk-forward and expanding. At each year-end y (1989…2018) a model is fitted for year y+1. Training rows
    are those whose label ends ≤ y-end, a purge of 22 sessions. Fitting goes through `fit_cv` with hand-built
    walk-forward `Split`s, which turns its leakage guards on.
  - Sizing: log σ̂ = log σ̂_zoo + 0.5·(log σ̂_GBM − log σ̂_zoo), the geometric mean, so "50% haircut" = half of the
    model's correction. Exposure = min(0.10/σ̂, 1.0) × L4 weights, exactly as L5.
  - A descriptive row without the haircut (raw GBM) is registered and counted.
  - Trade-off: it is a purely risk-side change on the same parent, so its ablation versus L5 is clean.
- **(b) The same, plus CPCV in development.** Add CPCV (6 groups, 2 test, purge 22) to pick `n_estimators` by early
  stopping. This is closer to "purged CV" in López de Prado (2018, AFML ch. 7, 12), but it adds a search, and a CPCV
  path uses future years to predict past ones, so it cannot drive a tradable book.
- **(c) Haircut = Harvey & Liu's rule of thumb.** Read "50% haircut" as the rule of thumb for halving a backtested
  Sharpe (Harvey & Liu 2015, JPM 42(1)) and report the raw-GBM book's Sharpe × 0.5. The sizing itself uses the raw
  model. This changes no position; quantkit marks `haircut_sharpe` as deprecated for decisions (CONSUMER_GUIDE).
- Sources: vol-managed sizing (Moreira & Muir 2017, JF 72(4)); ML vol forecasts beating HAR mostly via shrinkage
  (Christensen, Siggaard & Veliyev 2023, J. Fin. Econometrics 21(5)); LightGBM (Ke et al. 2017); purging and embargo
  (AFML ch. 7).
- **Data check:** none (O3.1).
- **Recommendation: (a).** Strongest reason: "purged" is satisfied by walk-forward splits whose labels never cross the
  refit date. That is the only CV that can size a real book. Fixed parameters keep the trial count at one.
  **Pushback:** "the haircut is arbitrary". Answer: it is the owner's number, fixed before any result. The raw row
  shows what it costs or saves.

## D4.4 — Scope of "robustness" and of the "loss waterfall per surviving rung"

- **(a) Run every robustness item on every built main rung.** No rung is promoted, so treat L0, L1, L2, L3, L4, L5,
  L5-lev, L6, L7 and L8 as "surviving" for descriptive robustness, labelled "no rung promoted". The items:
  - each layer versus its parent and versus the line at matched vol (D3.5) under 1×/2×/3× costs and +75 bp financing;
  - leave-one-crisis-out over the six development windows;
  - a decade table (1990–99, 2000–09, 2010–19);
  - rolling 5-year (1,260-session) excess Sharpe and ΔSharpe versus the matched parent;
  - a power table (`periods_needed` from each layer's measured development ΔSharpe and ρ, one-sided α 0.05, power
    0.8);
  - a waterfall per rung: annualised gross sleeve return + cash interest − SPY costs − TLT costs − financing = net;
    − bill = excess. It is additive and checked exactly against the book.
- **(b) Only the seat holders (L0, L1).** Trivial; it answers nothing about the layers.
- **(c) The core chain L3–L8 plus L2.** Drops v1 and the levered row.
- Sources: the charter's robustness list and "cost waterfall" chart; Memmel (2003) for power; matched-risk comparison
  per D3.5.
- **Recommendation: (a).** Strongest reason: the README must answer "did any layer beat …" for each layer, and the
  "surviving" filter is empty under every walk. **Pushback:** "robustness of rungs that failed is noise". Answer: it
  shows *how* they fail (costs or crises), which is the point of R4.

## D4.5 — Sensitivity grids: bond maturity, band width, calendar

- **(a) One factor at a time.**
  - Maturity: 10y, 20y and 30y instead of 25y on every rung holding TLT (L1, L3–L8).
  - Band width: 0, 2.5 and 10 pp versus the registered 5, monthly.
  - Calendar: quarterly (decisions at quarter-end rows only) versus monthly, 5 pp.
  - Band and calendar apply to L3–L7. The calendar acts on each rung's own month-end decision rows (mix, correlation,
    trend); the daily vol scaling stays daily.
  - Every variant is descriptive and enters the register (DSR N) before it is built.
- **(b) The full grid.** All 4 widths × 2 calendars × 5 rungs: 35 new books; more trials with little extra information.
- **(c) Maturity only on L3 and L5.** Cheaper, but it hides where duration matters (L6 in 1994).
- Sources: Jaconetti, Kinniry & Zilbering (2010, Vanguard, "Best practices for portfolio rebalancing") found monthly,
  quarterly and annual calendars and 1–10% bands give similar risk-adjusted results. Donohue & Yip (2003, JPM) and
  Leland (1999) show optimal no-trade bands widen with costs.
- **Recommendation: (a).** Strongest reason: it isolates each choice against the registered one with the fewest new
  trials. **Pushback:** "interactions (wide band × quarterly)". Answer: both are inert where turnover is low; the
  register can add the cross if the owner asks.

## D4.6 — DSR: which trials go into the effective N

- **(a) The full trial pool.** P = development daily bill-excess returns of every registered row with a return series:
  R1 L1; R2 rungs, zoo and tranche offsets; R3 rungs, descriptive rows and zoo; R4 zoo and sensitivity variants. It
  also includes **v1's 648-config grid re-run on the permitted data with charter accounting** (v1's selection came from
  it). Placebos are excluded (they are nulls, not candidates). N_eff comes from `stats.effective_trials` (ρ* 0.5,
  sensitivity as quantkit's default). `dsr_effective` is reported for each main rung and for the best Sharpe in the
  pool.
- **(b) Raw N = Σ n_configs in `variants.csv`.** This overstates N for near-duplicate trials (tranche offsets,
  ρ ≈ 0.99) and needs no returns.
- **(c) Pool (a) without v1's grid.** Cheaper, but it understates the search behind L1.
- Sources: Bailey & López de Prado (2014, JPM 40(5)); López de Prado & Lewis (2019, QF 19(9)); the charter's "DSR with
  the effective number of trials".
- **Recommendation: (a).** Strongest reason: the effective count needs the trials' returns, and v1's grid is the
  largest search in the project's history. **Pushback:** "re-running v1's grid on new data is a different search".
  Answer: yes. It measures how many independent bets that grid spans, and the count (648) is unchanged.

## D4.7 — PBO via CSCV: which pool and how many blocks

- **(a) The zoo pool.** Primary pool: L5 plus every zoo row built on it or beside it: L4b, the five fixed-vol-model L5
  rows, HMM gate, jump gate, min-CDaR, max-div, and R4's spread, Bollinger and GBM rows. Development daily excess
  returns, `stats.pbo(P, blocks=16)` (the example in Bailey et al. 2017). Descriptive: S = 8 and 12, and a second
  pool of every rung and sensitivity variant.
- **(b) One pool of everything.** This mixes near-duplicates and different risk levels; PBO is scale-free, but the pool
  is then dominated by tranche offsets.
- **(c) S = 10 primary.** The paper's examples use S = 16; fewer blocks means coarser halves.
- **Recommendation: (a).** Strongest reason: the charter says "PBO over the zoo". The secondary pool shows the wider
  selection without changing the primary.
