## D5.1 — How the frozen rules continue into 2020+

- **(a) The registered procedures run on unchanged.**
  - The vol-zoo MCS pick is re-selected at each year-end 2019 … 2025 for the next year: expanding, same seed, same
    order.
  - The L8 brake continues its path from 1990-01-02.
  - Books are continuous: positions are carried from 2019-12-31 and the test rows are scored.
  - Every rolling estimator keeps rolling.
- **(b) Freeze the state at 2019-12-31.** The 2019 pick (gjr_garch_t) serves every test year, with no re-selection.
- **(c) Restart every book at 2020-01-02** from cash, with fresh state.

**Sources:**
- Walk-forward evaluation treats the re-estimation schedule as part of the rule (Pardo 2008, *The Evaluation and
  Optimization of Trading Strategies*, ch. 11; López de Prado 2018, *AFML* §11–12). Freezing a fitted state is a
  different strategy than the one developed.
- Hansen, Lunde & Nason (2011, *Econometrica*): the MCS is re-run on the expanding sample by construction.

**Data check (development):** the pick was stable (4 changes in 30 years), so (a) and (b) are likely to differ little.
That is not a reason to change the registered procedure.

**Correctness check that (a) allows:** the extended books' rows ≤ 2019-12-31 must equal the R2/R3 books bit for bit.
Every transform is causal, so extending the sample must not move a development number.

**Recommendation: (a).** It is the registered rule, and it yields the bit-for-bit development check. The years listed
in rules.yaml (1989..2018) were the development range. R5's prereg block extends them to 2019..2025 before any test
number.

**Pushback:** "you re-selected the vol model with test data." The pick for year y uses losses realised ≤ (y−1)-end
only, which is the same causal rule as in development.

## D5.2 — "At matched risk" on the test window

- **(a) R3/R4's procedure applied to the scored window.**
  - Each reference is rescaled once, ex post, to the child's test-window excess vol, with bill + 25 bp above 1×
    (`evaluate.rescale`).
  - ΔSharpe comes with the LW 95% interval and the one-sided p; CDaR-95 and max-DD ratios come with the paired
    bootstrap.
  - The L0-line (ex ante, window 63, lag 1) is the charter benchmark, unchanged.
- **(b) Scale constants fixed from development** (k from 1990–2019 vols): ex ante with respect to the test.
- **(c) Both,** with (a) primary.

**Sources:**
- Matched-vol comparison is the standard way to compare Sharpe-equivalent books (Asness, Frazzini & Pedersen 2012, risk
  parity vs 60/40, scaled ex post to equal vol).
- The charter's line is the ex-ante benchmark.

**Data check (development):** development vol moves by decade (L0 14% → 22% → 15%, while L5 stays at 7–9%). A
development k would leave 2020+ books mismatched in vol (2020 and 2022 vol spikes), so (b)'s "matched" would not be
matched.

**Recommendation: (a).** It is the same procedure as R3/R4, and the line already supplies an ex-ante comparison.

**Pushback:** "ex-post matching peeks at the test vol." It is a single scalar, it is descriptive, it is the procedure
used in development, and the ex-ante line sits beside it.

## D5.3 — What the test run can conclude

- **(a) No verdict changes.** The development verdicts stand:
  - the R2 pre-registered walk seats L1;
  - the post-hoc chains seat L0;
  - R3 and R4 are descriptive.

  The test run answers the README's three questions (vs L2, vs 60/40 at matched vol, vs the line) and each layer vs
  its parent:
  - matched ΔSharpe with the LW 95% interval and the one-sided p;
  - "beat in the test window" = point ≥ +0.05 **and** one-sided p < 0.05, the charter's minimum effect and test (no MCS
    or legs on 6.75 years);
  - Holm p-values printed across the family;
  - every "beat" also shown with the power that 6.75 years gives.
- **(b) Re-run the four-part promotion walk on the test window.** This contradicts the charter ("Development … every
  ladder decision").
- **(c) Point estimates only,** with no test.

**Sources:**
- The charter windows table.
- Harvey & Liu (2015, "Backtesting") on the low power of short out-of-sample windows.
- R4 power table: no layer is confirmable in 6.75 years. L3 vs L0 needs 30.

**Recommendation: (a).** Its strongest reason is that it keeps the charter's split (development decides, test
reports) while still giving a registered yes/no.

**Pushback:** "then why run it?" To check sign and size against development, and to show 2022, the regime the thesis
is about. A test-window "no" is expected even for a real edge.

## D5.5 — Freeze contents and the run-once guard

- **(a) Lean lock, no tag.** `spec/freeze.lock.json` holds:
  - the SHA-256 of spec/{prereg,rules}.yaml, quantkit_pin.json and uv.lock;
  - every engine file (src/rspy/**/*.py, legacy/**/*.py, scripts/r5.py);
  - the raw-data manifest SHA-256s and the panel content hash;
  - rules_commit = HEAD and frozen_at.

  `python -m rspy freeze verify` checks it. The runner refuses unless:
  - every hash matches and the locked files are clean in git;
  - `RSPY_TEST_RUN=run-once` is set;
  - `reports/r5/test_run.json` does not exist yet (the run-once marker, committed with the results).

  No git tag: tags are the owner's.
- **(b) dispersion-reversal's full guard** (tags freeze-v1, a freeze-changes register, staged gates). Its tags are
  owner-only here, and it is heavier than a single-run SPY/TLT test.
- **(c) Hash the spec only.** Engine code could then change after the freeze without detection.

**Sources:** dispersion-reversal @ c09713a, and the charter performance and correctness contract.

**Dry run:** a seeded synthetic panel with the same columns and calendar (t(5) equity and bond returns, AR(1) bill)
under a temporary `RSPY_DATA`. The full R5 pipeline writes to a temporary output directory, never to reports/r5. It is
a code check, not evidence.

**Recommendation: (a).**

**Pushback:** "a lock without a tag can be rewritten." The lock is committed before the run. Its commit sha is printed
in the ledger and in test_run.json, and any later edit shows in git history.
