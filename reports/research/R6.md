## D6.1 — R6e, asymmetric vol targeting: weekly step-ups or a 5-day EWMA?
- **Options:**
  - (a) [rec] Primary row: the L2 scale is cut on any day the raw L2 scale falls below the scale held, and raised only
    at the last session of each calendar week, to that day's raw scale. Sensitivity row (the one allowed): step-ups via
    an EWMA with span 5 (α = 1/3) instead of weekly. Cuts are immediate in both.
  - (b) Weekly only.
  - (c) EWMA only.
- **Sources:**
  - Moreira & Muir (2017, *JF* 72(4)) and Harvey et al. (2018, *JPM* 45(1), "The Impact of Volatility Targeting") show
    that vol targeting's benefit comes from cutting exposure when volatility jumps. Harvey et al. find the left-tail
    reduction is the robust part.
  - Bongaerts, Kang & van Dijk (2020, *FAJ* 76(4), "Conditional Volatility Targeting") scale down only in high-vol
    states, an asymmetric rule.
  - No source fixes a re-entry delay. "Weekly" and "span 5" are the build prompt's own values.
- **Data check:** none. Choosing between them on development turnover would be tuning.
- **Why (a):** the prompt lists weekly first and names EWMA as the alternative. (a) shows both under the one-sensitivity
  rule, with weekly as the row of record.
- **Pushback:** weekly re-entry has day-of-week luck (Friday). It is disclosed and not swept.

## D6.2 — R6c, the faster correlation regime and the inflation-volatility gate
- **Options:**
  - (a) [rec] Two one-layer rows on L5.
    - R6c-63 = L6 with the 63-day correlation window. This is R3's registered dev row L6-63, unchanged, now also
      scored on the test.
    - R6c-infl = L5 with the inflation-volatility gate in place of the correlation gate: TLT goes to cash while the
      gate says high.
    - No combination row.
  - (b) One row: the 126-day correlation gate OR the inflation gate.
  - (c) (a) plus the combination "63-day OR inflation" as the one sensitivity row.
- **Inflation gate as written (prompt + owner O6.2):**
  - π_m = ln(CPI_m / CPI_{m−1}) on CPI-U NSA.
  - v_m = sample std (ddof 1) of the π over the 12 months ending at m.
  - The gate is high when v_m > the median of v over the 120 months ending at m (a strict ">").
- **Release lag:**
  - BLS publishes month m during month m+1, about mid-month. Even the shutdown-delayed September 2025 release came on
    2025-10-24.
  - So the month-end decision of month M uses months ≤ M−1.
  - This is the same decision row and next-close trade as L6.
- **The 2025-10 gap:** π for 2025-10 and 2025-11 is undefined. v_m needs ≥ 10 of its 12 π, and the median needs
  ≥ 100 of its 120 v. A decision without a defined v keeps the previous decision, through `rungs.carry`. The number
  of such decisions is counted and printed.
- **Why NSA:** it is never revised. Seasonal factors are revised for five years, so SA would not be point-in-time.
  Each 12-month window holds every calendar month once, so seasonality is a near-constant level in v.
- **Sources:**
  - Brixton, Brooks, Hecht, Ilmanen, Maloney & McQuinn (2023, *JPM* 49(4), "A Changing Stock–Bond Correlation: Drivers
    and Implications"): inflation uncertainty is the main driver of a positive stock–bond correlation.
  - Campbell, Pflueger & Viceira (2020, *JPE* 128(8)): the sign of the correlation follows the inflation regime.
  - Ilmanen (2003, *J. Fixed Income* 13(2)).
  - None fixes 12 / 120 / median. Those are the prompt's values.
- **Why (a):** each row adds one layer to a registered parent, the charter's rule. (b) mixes two changes in one row.
  (c) spends the one sensitivity row on a combination nobody asked for.
- **Pushback:** v uses one-month-old data, and 2021's inflation surge lifted v only from mid-2021. The gate may fire in
  2022 mostly because the idea was built from 2022. That is why the label "inspired by this window" exists.

## D6.3 — R6f, how the equal-weight blend is booked
- **Options:**
  - (a) [rec] A fund of three books.
    - Capital shares v_i (L2, L3, L7) reset to 1/3 on each month's first session (decided at the month-end close,
      traded t+1) and drift with each book's net return within the month.
    - Each sub-book keeps its own costs, financing and cash. No netting credit across books, which is conservative.
    - The reset trade is charged at the asset level: per asset, |Σ_i (1/3 − v_i) · w_i,asset| × 3 / 2 bp. Its
      turnover is added to Σ v_i · turnover_i.
  - (b) One book with daily target (1/3) Σ held_i through `accounting.run`. This nets trades but rebalances daily, not
    monthly, and its 5 pp band would act on the mix.
  - (c) A return blend with no reset cost.
- **Sources:**
  - DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal (2009, *RFS* 22(5)): 1/N combinations of strategies.
  - The prompt fixes "one-third each, rebalanced monthly".
- **Why (a):** it is the prompt's rule as written, charter costs on true traded turnover, and conservative.
- **Pushback:** in one real account the three books' trades would partly net, so (a) overstates costs slightly. The
  2× cost row bounds it anyway.

## D6.4 — Comparators, matching, the common-sense verdict and DSR N
- **Options:**
  - (a) [rec] Each candidate has one "own parent" for the verdict:
    - R6a → L2; R6b rows → the same rung at 25y; R6c-63 → L6; R6c-infl → L5 (L6 shown beside); R6d → L5;
      R6e → L2; R6f → the line.
    - The blend has no single parent. L2, L3 and the line are all shown, and the verdict uses the line, the charter's
      benchmark.
    - **ΔSharpe vs L2 / L3 / the line** go into every row, with LW 95% intervals.
    - **Matching:** the reference is rescaled once, ex post, to the candidate's excess vol in that window (development:
      R3 D3.5; test: R5 D5.2).
    - **Verdict per window:** "better" if ΔSharpe vs own parent ≥ +0.05 (the charter's minimum effect), "worse" if
      ≤ −0.05, else "about the same". "Clearly" is added when the 95% interval excludes 0.
    - **The pair maps to fixed words:**
      - better / better → "better in both periods"
      - same-or-worse / better → "better only in the period that inspired it"
      - better / same → "better in development only"
      - better / worse → "better in development, worse in the test"
      - same / same → "no real difference"
      - worse in one and not better in the other → "worse"
    - **R6e also gets** its turnover change in % beside the words.
    - **DSR:** every R6 row is counted. Test-window DSR uses N_headline = 5 + n_R6 and N_raw = 751 + n_R6, with R5's
      trial-variance rule. Development DSR uses R4's n_used 4 + n_R6. R6 rows go into variants.csv (counted yes)
      before computing. Rows already counted earlier (L6-63, the 10-year rows) are counted again, conservatively, as
      in the R5 precedent.
  - (b) Verdict on the LW interval only (excludes 0 or not). On ≈ 7 test years almost nothing clears it (R4 power), so
    every verdict would read "no difference".
  - (c) A Holm-adjusted family over R6 rows. That is a formal test the owner did not ask for: "common sense".
- **Why (a):** the charter's own minimum effect, the R3 / R5 matching conventions, and words that say plainly when the
  gain sits only in the window that suggested it.
- **Pushback:** ±0.05 on point estimates is noisy. The intervals are printed beside every verdict.
